# A Journey Through The Lens

### The Two Sides of the Image
![Alt text](/images/mystoryimage.avif)
For over twenty years, I have lived a life defined by images. By day, as a Diagnostic Radiographer and Sonographer within the NHS, I use high-tech imaging to look "within" the body for clarity. But for just as long, my camera has been my personal compass—a way to navigate the world and find my place in it.


### Burnout, Discovery, and Reinvention 
After a career in high-stakes healthcare, I experienced a period of profound burnout—the result of a lifetime of high masking and undiagnosed Autism and ADHD. This “ending” became my beginning. My diagnosis finally decoded my world, validating my obsession with the intricate and explaining why the camera has always been my most natural way of communicating with the world.

This revelation changed everything. It reframed my intense eye for detail, my "alternative" sense of humour, and my deep, sensory need to create. I realised that my way of using photography—as a tool for grounding and sensory processing—was a gift I could share with others. This journey taught me that photography isn't just about "taking a picture"; it’s a vital anchor for the neurodivergent mind.


### Finding Mindfulness in the Chaos
While many associate mindfulness with silent landscapes, I find my “stillness” within the vibrant energy of the world and its inherent chaos.
‍
- **Street & Stage:** I find a profound flow state in the kinetic energy of street photography and the raw immersion of live music. These environments demand presence; the noise of the external world falls away, leaving only the light and the rhythm. Capturing the raw emotion of a performance requires a focus so intense it silences all internal chatter. It is a sensory overload that, paradoxically, brings me clarity.
‍
- **The Heart of the Home:** Perhaps my most vital work happens within the quiet, complicated layers of family life. In seasons of caregiving and through the complex hurdles we face together, the camera acts as my anchor and my outlet. It serves as a way to process the weight of difficult emotions, allowing me to distill overwhelming moments into something tangible and beautiful. By focusing on the spark in a loved one’s eyes or the uninhibited joy of my nephew at play, I use my lens to look past the heavy surroundings and honour the love that keeps us moving forward. To me, photography is a way to pause the clock, transforming seasons of struggle into a gallery of enduring hope and resilience.

## A New Calling
Having trained in Therapeutic Photography with Dr. Neil Gibson and Exposure Therapy with Gabriella Motola, I am now merging my clinical expertise with my creative heart. I am dedicated to showing others that the camera can be a bridge to well-being. My work at Bridgend Farmhouse and within the hospital is about teaching people to look closer, breathe deeper, and find the "gold" in their own stories—no matter how difficult the environment.
